## Purpose

This document will hold the scenario-based business plan for the content and
AI-media business (CONTENT-004): turning the research capability into a
trading-platform brand through AI-generated media. It defines the audience,
value proposition, operating model, costs, revenue paths, and — critically —
the kill criteria that end the venture if scenarios fail. All scenarios and
every number in them are defined in Round M; this skeleton contains no revenue
figures, and none may be invented before then.

## Scope and decisions this document will own

- The business scenarios (defined in Round M) and the assumptions each rests
  on, stated explicitly and separately from evidence.
- The go/no-go milestones and kill criteria for the content business.
- The build-versus-buy position for each operating capability.
- Out of scope: product workflow, character, compliance, and publishing
  detail — owned by the sibling documents in this folder.

## Structure skeleton

### 1. Goal and strategic fit

Plain-language statement of the goal: use AI media to build a brand around the
research capability. This section must also reconcile the goal with D-008
(AutoFX itself serves Jacob only — no customers, subscribers, or copy-trading),
so exactly what is offered to an audience is an open Round M decision, not an
assumption.

### 2. Audience

Who the content serves, in which countries, and what problem it solves for
them. Open Round M questions shared with CONTENT_STUDIO_PRODUCT_SPEC.md;
country choice also feeds Q-006 jurisdiction review.

### 3. Value proposition

What the brand offers that alternatives do not, grounded in the platform's
evidence-honesty stance (BUS-004: uncertainty always reported, profitability
never guaranteed — including in marketing). Defined in Round M.

### 4. Competitive landscape

Survey of comparable finance/trading content brands and products, with dated
sources. Round M research; no competitor claims without evidence.

### 5. Operating model

How the business runs day to day: roles (Jacob-only vs. any delegation),
production cadence, tooling, and dependency on the Content Studio pipeline.
Round M, with CHANNEL_PUBLISHING_PLAN.md.

### 6. Build versus buy

For each capability (media generation, editing, scheduling, analytics):
build, buy, or subscribe — assessed only after the provider evaluations in
[AI_CHARACTER_AND_BRAND_SPEC.md](AI_CHARACTER_AND_BRAND_SPEC.md) return dated
evidence. Round M.

### 7. Cost model

Cost categories (provider subscriptions, tooling, any legal review, media
licences) with amounts filled in only from dated quotes/pricing evidence in
Round M. No estimates are invented here.

### 8. Revenue paths

Candidate monetisation routes (e.g. platform monetisation, and whether
affiliation or offers are acceptable at all) — enumerated and assessed in
Round M. Each path must clear CONTENT_COMPLIANCE_AND_APPROVAL.md and must not
contradict D-008. **No revenue numbers are invented; scenarios are defined in
Round M.**

### 9. CAC/LTV assumptions

The assumption set behind any customer-acquisition and lifetime-value
reasoning, recorded as explicit assumptions with sources — only once Round M
defines the offer and scenarios. Empty until then.

### 10. Scenario definitions and break-even

The scenario framework (e.g. conservative/base/ambitious — structure itself to
be confirmed) with per-scenario assumptions and break-even logic. Entirely
Round M; no scenario numbers exist yet.

### 11. Risks

Business risks: platform dependency, policy change, compliance exposure,
provider withdrawal, brand/reputation damage from synthetic media, and the
core risk that trading evidence may not support strong public claims
(BUS-004). Register drafted in Round M.

### 12. Milestones and kill criteria

Ordered milestones from planning to first publish and beyond, each gated on
its prerequisites (P1 live-validated per D-010; compliance sign-off per
CONTENT-003). Kill criteria: the explicit conditions under which the content
business is stopped. Both defined and owner-approved in Round M (CONTENT-004).
